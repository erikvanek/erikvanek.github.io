# Product Strategy

- There are reversible and irreversible product decisions – reversible are easy to iterate and tweak once in production, irreversible should be thought through during the product development lifecycle
- *in good companies the strategy and the story are the same thing thus every strategy produced there is a story and the story of a company goes beyond quarterly and yearly goals and answers around the question "why" → why should I join the company? why should I be excited to work here? why should I buy its product? why is a world better off thanks to what this company produces? ...* ([[The Hard Thing About Hard Things#^hthat-strategy-story|Horowitz]])
- *great CEOs create exceptional strategies that allow them to continuously build internal intelligence to support good decision making* ([[The Hard Thing About Hard Things#^hthat-great-ceos|Horowitz]])

## Good strategy vs bad strategy

- in his book *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy*, Richard Rumelt describes good strategy as having "coherence, coordinating actions, policies, and resources so as to accomplish an important end"
- he goes on to describe how many organisations don't have this, but instead have "multiple goals and initiatives that symbolise progress"
- to achieve real progress you need a strategy, and to achieve that, **ruthlessly prioritising your activities is key**
- **put really simply, strategy is to get from where we are now (our problem statement) to where we want to be (our vision)** - which makes [[Problem framing]] and [[Product Vision]] the two ends of a strategy
