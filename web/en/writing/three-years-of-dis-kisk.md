May 2026. [UX Monday](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/cile-VI/#ux-monday) in Prague, the biggest design meetup in the country. I had 45 minutes to talk about how I work. I prepared a large part of the talk with a method I now call the “braindump method”: I walked around the city and recorded a long voice message about what I actually wanted to get across.

That moment sums up the road I’ve travelled over the last three years. The steps picked for this portfolio show the milestones of my studies in the DIS KISK programme at Masaryk University.

## How did I start?

Behind me were almost ten years in software and a fresh layoff from Infarm, the end of one chapter. I entered [DIS](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/erik-2023/) as more of an engineer than a designer. I had plenty of practice, but I did it by feel: I _“pressed buttons”_ and watched what worked. An engineering base, strong opinions, gaps in methodology. Going back to study at KISK gave me welcome support in the career change I set out on after Infarm. After years of glancing in this direction, my goal was to become a designer _for real_.

## Step one: a friend at the hackathon

First semester, the final [hackathon](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/hackaton-I/). I invited a friend to our usability testing, a teacher who until then had nothing to do with design. He helped us test a tool that could serve primary schools as an internal competence audit, mapping where they could develop next.

A few months later he decided to run for headteacher of a primary school. And he won. He told me afterwards that taking part in our hackathon was the final push that made him apply.

This was my first taste of what I still love about design: a small intervention that opens a door for someone, and the door stays open long after the project ends. Today he is finishing his second year as headteacher, and a school with a bad reputation is turning into one that attracts teachers from schools you could call “selective”.

## Step two: Fléda and a friendliness audit

A year later I decided to approach [Fléda](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/fleda-audit/) about a project, a Brno music club I go to myself and in some ways one of the most important cultural venues in the city. I had met Fléda’s CEO a few times over lunch with friends, but we barely knew each other. I had no contact, so I guessed the format of the company email address and wrote to him cold. The next day we were sitting together, I was explaining what I could do for him, and he liked the idea.

We ran a friendliness audit of the whole venue: interviews with the people who work there, a survey with hundreds of responses, recommendations for what to do next. When you come to Fléda today, some of those things are really appearing there, one by one, which makes me very happy.

At the same time, on a public sector contract, I led a [usability study of Jarmilka, a digital guide](/writing/mum-wont-switch-it-off/), which later became my bachelor’s thesis. I only mention it here; the full story is in its own article.

## Step three: from research to speaking in public

This step is really a giant leap, and it starts in the second semester. With our research team, the Drýmtým, we [looked at how innovative teams in the Czech public administration work](https://www.inovacnitymystatnispravy.cz/). We talked to people who help innovate this country’s public services systematically, and published a digital study.

The research didn’t stay in a drawer. As sometimes happens, a chain of coincidences put it on the desk of the Top State Secretary, the person who runs the Czech civil service. After a meeting we arranged with him, in 2025 we brought [the state secretaries of more than half of the Czech ministries](/writing/ministry-secretaries-workshop/) into one room: an ideation workshop and a space to spark thinking about innovation with people who have a real chance to bring it into public services.

Meanwhile the [Neighbourhood Children’s Groups](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/sousedske-detske-skupiny/) project I worked on won the Ministry of the Interior’s medal for civil service achievement of the year. At Zlín Design Week I [talked about design in public administration on the Design KANTÝNA podcast](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/ucast-na-konferencich/), my first time in front of a professional audience.

This whole step, more of a stride really, led to me [teaching](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/vyuka/) design as well. I learned to teach step by step too: first as a tutor alongside Matěj Káninský, then solo with a service design workshop for KISK students, and finally the hardest one, [KISKED19](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/praxe-V/), a design thinking course for civil servants, the people working in the field.

The sixth semester didn’t add any big new turns to my studies. Everything just came together nicely.

I built an [agentic workflow](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/cile-VI/) that now runs my whole design process: a second brain in Obsidian, MCP servers, Claude Code, Markdown as the lingua franca. Part of my working time moved from the chair to walks, where I dictate things and process them afterwards at the computer with the [braindump method](https://www.erikvanek.com/dis-kisk/ux-monday-braindump/): I walk, talk into my phone, and come back to sorted material.

There’s no magic to it. Garbage in, garbage out holds more and more, and checking the output is work that doesn’t go away. With this workflow I prepared the UX Monday talk and this portfolio too.

## My studies, step by step

In 2023 I entered the programme as more of an engineer than a designer: practice by feel, strong opinions, gaps in methodology. Going step by step helped me grow my professional confidence. If someone had told me three years ago that during my studies I’d be part of a team that gets the leadership of half the Czech ministries into one room and runs a great workshop on innovation for them, I wouldn’t have believed it. But it happened, and I’m immensely grateful for it.

Here is a more structured overview of my studies: six semesters, six concrete shifts on a journey that certainly doesn’t end with graduation.
