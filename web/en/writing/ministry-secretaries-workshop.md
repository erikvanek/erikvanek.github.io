In the spring semester of 2024 our team was finishing **research on innovative teams in the Czech state administration**, the project that carried my second semester at KISK. We finalised it at the end of 2024 and [published it online](https://www.inovacnitymystatnispravy.cz/) early this year. It was done and filed away, and I was looking forward to finally catching my breath. At the same time, the Drýmtým and I were thinking about how to build on it, given how much effort we had put in.

Then one of those things happened that you just can’t plan. Someone met someone, and our research landed in the hands of **Jindřich Fryč, the Top State Secretary for the civil service**, the person who in practice runs the whole Czech state administration. And instead of reading it and filing it away, he invited us to a meeting in person.

## Make your own opportunity!

At the meeting we talked about what we had found. It made sense to us and to the Top State Secretary to build on the research and introduce it, in a practical way, to the people he works with. **So we proposed running a workshop** based on what our research found.

[LinkedIn post about our meeting with the Top State Secretary](https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7287950756219928578/)

He liked the idea, and we quickly agreed to hold a workshop in May for **the ministries’ state secretaries and a chosen +1 each**. In a way that’s the ideal scenario, because our own research shows that **change in the public sector needs support from the top**. And we would have a great chance to spend a day under one roof with the leadership of most of the Czech ministries.

A few key things came out of the meeting:

- The workshop will build directly on our research and its findings.
- Participants will come from different ministries, from finance through health to the interior.
- The goal is to create space for **sharing good practice across ministries**.
- Format: 3 hours, with the emphasis on practical outputs people take away.

## The most demanding preparation of my life

Then the preparation started. And I have to say that **this was the most detailed professional thing I have ever prepared**. Every minute was planned, every question thought through and run through several iterations, every transition between activities rehearsed. All in the spirit of the 5 Ps: proper planning prevents poor performance.

Following good design practice, our work with the Top State Secretary’s team was **iterative**: several rounds of feedback, tuning the activities, refining every detail. We mostly kept the initiative on our side and guided his team through our process. I realised how different working with stakeholders from public administration is compared to the commercial world or NGOs.

One of the nice artefacts that came out of the preparation was a **printed booklet** with the script and materials for every participant, something they could take home and maybe use with their own teams.

For the workshop itself we chose a variation on the **World café** format, with three topics drawn from our research:

1. **Hiring and keeping talent**: how to attract good people to the civil service
2. **Networking and relationships**: building collaboration inside and between organisations
3. **The learning organisation**: how to support growth and sharing know-how

The second half of the workshop focused on **“small steps”**, so that every participant left with a concrete action they could start on the next day.

## D-Day

5 May 2025 (an easy date to remember) was our D-Day. Hotel Spiritka, a room in the former stables, a hall full of state secretaries, **the Top State Secretary taking an active part for all three hours**, an atmosphere that was friendly and focused.

What surprised me most? **How naturally people from different ministries started working together.** They swapped contacts, found shared challenges and inspired each other. One person shared a successful hire through an unusual channel, another described a mentoring programme that works.

In the second part, as everyone picked their “small step”, I watched concrete ideas being born: a shared contact database across ministries, regular informal breakfasts for innovators, a pilot exchange programme between ministries. Or an “hour a week for learning” for every team member, better onboarding checklists for managers, and joint cross-ministry events like a “health day” or “civil servants for civil servants”.

## What I took away

My biggest finding? **Collaboration across ministries is possible, and with enough preparation and good facilitation it can bring very interesting ideas and an atmosphere that even quite senior people appreciate.** Design really can be a strong tool for connecting people across ministries.

As moderators, it was **important to us that everything ran to the script**. My role was to make sure everything ran on time, that participants understood what was going to happen, that they were happy and that everything worked as it should, and to moderate the whole day.

What worked:

- **Thorough preparation**: every detail mattered.
- **Flexibility within the structure**: stick to the script, and be able to respond to the group’s dynamics.
- **Active participation “from the top”**: the Top State Secretary’s presence gave the event weight and set a good tone for the participants.

It also confirmed for me that facilitation is less about techniques and methods than about reading the group, creating a safe space and **the art of giving every voice room**. Although I wasn’t in a facilitation role myself, I liked that I could also learn from how my colleagues who ran the individual tables approach facilitation.

## What’s next?

Above all, this workshop confirms where I want to grow as a facilitator. I want to work on projects with the potential to **really change how organisations work together**. And I was pleased, as I believe the whole team was, by how participants reacted after the event and by the feedback we got in the follow-up form.
